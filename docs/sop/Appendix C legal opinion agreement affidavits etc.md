#  3 OUR COMMENTS

##  3.1 Section 194LA of the IT Act

3.1.1 Section 194L.A of the IT Act provides for deduction of tax at source at the rate of 10 per cent of the amount paid as compensation/ consideration for acquisition of immovable property. The following are the essentials for the deduction of tax at source under section 194L.A:

(a) Payment of compensation/ consideration should be made to a resident of India.

(b) Such payment should be on account of 'compulsory acquisition' of immovable property under any law for the time being in force






amarchand mangaldas

(c) The immovable property acquired should not be 'agricultural land' as defined under section 194 LA of the IT Act

(d) The aggregate amount of compensation/ consideration paid during a financial year should be more than INR 2,00,000/-

3.1.2 In view of the above, the most important requirement for invocation of section 194LA of the IT Act is that the payment of consideration should have been made on account of 'compulsory acquisition' under any law for the time being in force. However, the term 'compulsory acquisition' has not been defined in the IT Act; therefore, we draw reference from the dictionary meaning and judicial analysis of the expression. Reliance in this regard is placed on the case of CIT, West Bengal v. Benoy Kumar Sahus Roy [1957] 32 ITR 466 (SC), wherein the Hon'ble Supreme Court has observed that when a term is not defined in a statute its dictionary meaning may be considered to understand the general meaning ascribed to such term. It was held thus:

"The term 'agriculture' and 'agricultural purpose' not having been defined in the Indian Income-tax Act, we must necessarily fall back upon the general sense in which they have been understood in common parlance. 'Agriculture' in its root sense means ager a field, and culture, cultivation, cultivation of a field which of course implies expenditure of human skill and labour upon land. The term has, however, acquired a wider significance and that is to be found in the various dictionary meanings ascribed to it. It may be permissible to look to the dictionary meaning of the term in the absence of any definition thereof in the relevant statutes."

3.1.3 The above principle has been reiterated by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in M/s Star Paper Mills Ltd. v. Collector of Central Excise, Meerut 1989 3 SCR 892 SC.

3.2 Meaning of 'compulsory acquisition' & 'compulsory'

3.2.1 Webster's Comprehensive Dictionary of the English Language, 2003 Edition, defines the word 'compulsory' to mean "required by law or other rule; enforced".

3.2.2 Oxford English Dictionary, Volume I, defines the word 'compulsory' to mean "a legal mandate compelling obedience".

3.2.3 Black's Law Dictionary, Seventh Edition, defines the word compulsory as "mandated by legal process or by statute"

3.2.4 In Ilalsbury's Laws of England, the expression 'compulsory acquisition' has been judically analysed as follows:





“Where land or an interest in land is purchased or taken under statutory powers without the agreement of the owner it is said to have been compulsorily acquired, but where there is statutory power to take mere possession of the land without the acquisition of any estate or interest in it apart from the possession, it is said to have been requisitioned."

3.2.5 In the case of State of Gujarat v. Shantilal Mungaldas and Ors. AIR 1969 SC 634, a Constitutional Bench of the Hon’ble Supreme Court while interpreting the provisions of Clause 2 and 2A of Article 31 of the Constitution of India explained the requirements of ‘compulsory acquisition’ in the following words:

"22. The following principles emerge from an analysis of Clauses (2) and (2A): compulsory acquisition or requisition may be made for a public purpose alone, and must be made by authority of law. Law which deprives a person of property, but does not transfer ownership of the property or right to possession of the property to the State or a corporation owned or controlled by the State is not a law for compulsory acquisition or requisition. The law, under the authority of which property is compulsorily acquired or requisitioned, must either fix the amount of compensation or specify the principles on which, and the manner in which, the compensation is to be determined and given."

3.2.6 In the case of Sunder v. Union of India (2001) 7 SCC 211, the Hon'ble Supreme Court explained the distinction between 'compulsory acquisition' of land and voluntary sale/transfer in the following manner:

Compulsory nature of acquisition is to be distinguished from voluntary sale or transfer. In the latter, the landowner has the widest advantage in finding out a would-he buyer and in negotiating with him regarding the sale price. Even in such negotiations or huggling, normally no landowner would bargain for any amount in consideration of his disinclination to part with the land. The mere fact that he is negotiating for sale of the land would show that he is willing to part with the land. The owner is free to settle terms of transfer and choose the buyer as also to appoint the point of time when he would be receiving consideration and parting with his title and possession over the land. But in the compulsory acquisition the landowner is deprived of the right and opportunity to negotiate and bargain for the sale price. It depends on what the Collector or the court fixes as per the provisions of the Act."








3.2.7 Similarly, in the case of Income Tax Officer (TDS) Ruipur C.G. v. Naya Ruipur Development Authority$^{5}$, the Chbattisgarh High Court has discussed at length the meaning of 'compulsory acquisition' in the context of section 194LA of the IT Act. The relevant observations of the Hon'ble High Court have been reproduced below:

"16. It is clear that in a case of compulsory acquisition, the seller has no option. He can neither refuse to sell his land nor can he negotiate the price. The price is fixed by the statute itself. The conditions that must be satisfied before a purchase can be said to be compulsory acquisition are as follows:

(i) The seller has no option but to sell the land;

(ii) The seller can not negotiate the price. It is fixed by the statute or determined under the principles mentioned therein.

17. There are many examples of such statutes. The Zamindart Abolition Acts, Ceiling Acts, Nationalisation Acts are examples of such compulsory acquisition, but the most prominent law regarding compulsory acquisition of immovable property is the LA-Act."

"22. The Collector fixes the compensation under section 11 of the LA-Act. He can take possession after award is made and the property vests in the State without any encumbrances under section 16 of the LA-Act."

"24. The compensation required to be given under the LA-Act is the market value of the property on the date of the notification under section 4. It is to be calculated by the principles mentioned under section 23 of the LA-Act."

"26. Under the LA-Act, there is no discretion of a person not to sell the land or to negotiate the price. The price is fixed under the principles mentioned under the LA-Act. This is a compulsory acquisition."

3.2.8 In view of the above dictionary meanings and judicial analysis, the requirements for 'compulsory acquisition' can be summarized as follows:

(a) The acquisition of land should be mandated under a statute

(b) The seller of the land should not have any discretion to negotiate the consideration to be paid for the acquisition of land

(c) The amount of consideration should be fixed by the statute or determined under the principles, mentioned therein

#  3.4 Acquisition under LA 1894:

3.4.1 Section 11 of the LA 1894 empowers the Collector to fix the compensation for the acquisition of land. Further, section 23 of LA 1894 provides for certain matters to be considered by the court in determining the amount of compensation to be awarded for the land acquired under LA 1894.

3.4.2 A perusal of these provisions would show that under the LA 1894, the person whose land is acquired does not have any discretion to negotiate the acquisition consideration as the same is determined as per the principles mentioned in section 23 of the LA 1894.

3.4.3 Therefore, in our view, the requirements for 'compulsory acquisition' as set out in paragraph 3.2.8 above are met and accordingly if the other requirements of section 194LA of the IT Act as set out in paragraph 3.1.1 above are also met, then ECL may be liable to deduct tax at source at the prescribed rate before making payment of the compensation/ consideration to the concerned land owner.

#  3.5 Acquisition under LARRA 2013:

3.5.1 LARRA 2013 was enacted by the legislature and came into effect from January 1, 2014 repealing LA 1894.

3.5.2 LARRA 2013 provides for acquisition of land by the appropriate government both for a private company and a public sector undertaking. Where land is acquired for a private company for a public purpose, prior consent of at least 80 per cent of the affected families as defined in sub clauses (i) and (v) of clause (c) of section 3 is required. However, no such requirement is prescribed where land is acquired for a public sector undertaking. In the instant case we







understand that ECL is a public sector undertaking and accordingly the above consent provisions will not be applicable to it.

3.5.3 Section 11" of LARRA 2013 provides that for the purpose of acquisition of land under LARRA 2013, whenever a land is proposed to be acquired for any public purpose, a preliminary notification shall be issued to that effect providing the details of such land in the manner prescribed in section 11. Section 26" of LARRA 2013 provides the criteria for determination of the market value of the land to be so acquired by the Collector and section 28" provides the parameters to be considered by the collector in determination of the amount of compensation to be awarded to the land owners. Further, section 30" of LARRA 2013 provides for a 'solatium' amount equal to the compensation amount to be paid to land owners whose land is acquired under LARRA 2013 in addition to the compensation determined by the Collector.

3.5.4 Furthermore, sub-section (1) of section $37^{14}$ of LARRA 2013 provides that the award of the Collector shall be final and conclusive evidence of the true area and market value of the land and the assets attached thereto, the amount of compensation and the solatium amount so determined.

3.5.5 A perusal of these provisions would show that under LARRA 2013, the person whose land is acquired does not have any discretion to negotiate the acquisition consideration as the same is determined as per the principles mentioned under sections 26 and 28 of LARRA 2013.

3.5.6 It is pertinent to mention here that the Central Government, in exercise of their powers under clauses (a), (c), (d), (i) and (u) of Sub-section (2) of Section 109 of the LARRA 2013 has made the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement (Social Impact Assessment and Consent) Rules, 2014 ("Rules"). Section 111 of the LARRA 2013 confers powers on the State Government to make rules under the LARRA 2013 as well. However from the inquiries made by us, we find that as on date, the State Governments of Jharkhand and West Bengal, where TCL has operative mines, have not formulated any rules under LARRA 2013. Thus, the operative mines of ECI, located within the States of Jharkhand and West Bengal which are acquired under the LARRA 2013, shall be guided by the Rules in the absence of any rules framed by the respective State Governments under LARRA 2013.

Therefore, in our view, the conditions of 'compulsory acquisition' as set out in paragraph 3.2.8 above are met and accordingly if the other requirements of section 194LA of the IT Act as set out in paragraph 3.1.1 above are also met, ECL may be liable to deduct tax at source at the prescribed rate before making payment of the compensation/ consideration to the concerned land owner.

In case where land is purchased directly from the land owner through a sale deed, the sale price of the land will naturally be determined/ fixed upon negotiation with the land owner and the land owner is not under obligation to sell the land on the sale price determined by any statute.









therefore, the requirements of 'compulsory acquisition' as set out in paragraph 3.2.8 will not be set. Accordingly, ECL may take a stand that the provisions of Section 194LA of the IT Act will not be applicable.

#  .7 Acquisition of land against compensation paid under section 14(1) of CBA 1957

8.7.1 Section $4^{15}$ of the CBA 1957 provides for a preliminary notification to be issued by the Central Government for giving notice in respect of its intention to prospect coal in any land where coal is likely to be obtained. Section $7^{16}$ of the CBA 1957 empowers the Central Government to acquire any land or rights in or over any land notified in section 4 by issuing a notification to that effect within the prescribed period. Section $11^{17}$ of the CBA 1957 provides for land to be acquired under the CBA 1957 to be vested in a Government Company if the Central Government is satisfied that such Government Company is willing to comply or has complied with such terms and conditions as the Central Government may impose in that respect.

3.7.2 Sub-section (5) of section $13^{18}$ of the CBA 1957 provides the parameters to be taken into consideration while determining the compensation to be paid to the land owners.

3.7.3 However, sub-section (1) of section $14^{19}$ of the CBA 1957 provides that where the amount of compensation to be paid under CBA 1957 can be fixed by agreement between the parties, such compensation shall be paid in accordance with such agreement. We have been informed that ECL has been acquiring land under section 11 of the CBA with the compensation to be paid determined vide agreement under sub section (1) of section 14 of the CBA.

3.7.4 Therefore, under this mode of acquisition of land, the land owner retains the right to negotiate the sale price of the land to be acquired and accordingly, the conditions for 'compulsory acquisition' as set out in paragraph 3.2.8 are not met. Accordingly, ECL may take a stand that the provisions of Section 194LA of the IT Act will not be applicable.

3.7.5 However, it is pertinent to note here that if the requirements of section 194-1A of the IT Act as set out in paragraph 3.3.1 above are met, ECL may be liable to deduct tax at source under the said section at the prescribed rate before making payment to the concerned land owner.

3.7.6 It is important to note here that by virtue of the Ordinance, with effect from January 1, 2015 the provisions of LARRA 2013 dealing with determination of compensation will also apply to CBA 1957. Therefore, even though acquisition of land will continue to happen under CBA 1957, the compensation for such acquisition will be determined as per the provisions of LARRA 2013,









since the amendments made to Section 105(3) of the LARRA 2013 by the Ordinance shall have the effect of implied repeal of the relevant provisions of CBA 1957 which deals with determination of compensation. As mentioned above, Section 13 and 14 of CBA 1957 lays down the compensation mechanism for any acquisition of land under CBA 1957 and thus in view of the Ordinance coming into effect from January 1, 2015, the provisions contained in the said Sections 13 and 14 shall cease to have effect in respect of any acquisition of land under CBA 1957. As discussed in paragraph 3.5 above, the compensation mechanism under LARRA 2013 does not provide for determination of compensation by way of negotiation/ agreement between the parties. Therefore, in case any land is acquired under the provisions of CBA 1957 read with the compensation provisions provided under LARRA 2013, the tax deduction implications will be same as those discussed in para 3.5.7 above.

An opinion has been sought for from me by the Queriest as to who are the persons entitled to one time payment of 5 lakhs Rupees per affected family against acquisition of land under the Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition and Development Act), 1957 where compensation award has been published as per the Schedules of the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 and has not been created.

I have gone through the brief for opinion sent to me for my opinion and also discussed the matter with the officers and the Advocate attending the conference.

The short facts necessary for furnishing my opinion are not in dispute. The queriest is a subsidiary company of Coal India Limited and a Central PSU under the Ministry of Coal, Government of India. The quereist has acquired large areas of land for its coal mining projects under the Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition & Development), Act 1957 (in short the CBA(A&D)) Act, 1957 in the Districts of Godda and Pakur. Acquisitions of land under the said Act are in progress in the district of Dumka for its newly allocated Greenfield Mines. I am informed that at several places compensation awards for land owners have already been published as per the 1st Schedule of the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 (in short the RFCTLARR Act, 2013. At some places the determination of compensation for taking physical possession of the acquired lands are in progress. Further in terms of the provisions contained in the IInd Schedule relating to Sl. No. 4 i.e choice of annuity as per clause (b) is also coming up for consideration. However in absence




of clarity and in absence of broad guidelines as to who would be the person entitled to receive on time payment of 5 Lakhs Rupees per “affected family”, clarity is lacking.

The $II^{nd}$ Schedule to the RFCTLARR Act, 2013 provides for the elements of rehabilitation and Resettlement, Entitlements for all the “affected families” ( both land owners and families whose livelihood is primarily depended on land acquired), in addition to those provided in the $I^{st}$ Schedule . SI No. 4 of the $II^{nd}$ Schedule deals with choice of annuity or employment and provides that the appropriate government shall ensure that the affected families with the following options:-

a. $\ldots$

b. On time payment of 5 lakhs rupees per affected family; or

C. $\ldots$

The expression "affected family" is defined in Section 3(c) of the RFCTLARR Act, 2013 and includes –

i. A family whose land or other immovable property has been acquired.

ii. A family which does not own any land but a member or members of such family may be agricultural Laborers, tenants including any form of tenancy or holding of Usufruct Right, Share-Croppers or artisans or who may be working in the affected area for 3 years prior to the acquisition of the land, whose primarily source of livelihood stand affected by the acquisition of land.

iii. The Schedule Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers who have lost any of their forest rights recognized under the Schedule Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 due to acquisition of land.

iv. Family whose primary source of livelihood for 3 years prior to the acquisition of the land is depended on forests or water bodies and includes gatherers of forest produce, hunters, fisher folk and boat men and such livelihood is affected due to acquisition of land.





v. A member of the family who has been assigned land by the State government or the Central Government under any of its schemes and such land is under acquisition.

vi. A family residing on any land in the urban areas for presiding 3 years or more prior to the acquisition of the land or whose primarily source of livelihood for 3 years prior to the acquisition of the land is affected by the acquisition of such land.

Further section 3 (k) defines "displaced family" and means any family, who on account of acquisition of land has to be relocated and resettled from the affected area to the resettlement area.

Section 3(m) defines “family” and includes a person, his or her spouse, minor children, minor brothers and minor sisters dependent on him.

Provided that widows, divorcees and women deserted by families shall be considered “separate families”.

Explanation:- an adult of either gender with or without spouse or children or dependents shall be considered as a separate family for the purposes of this Act.

Section 3(r) defines “Land owner” and includes any person :-

i. Whose name is recorded as the owner of the land or building or part thereof, in the records of the authority concerned; or

ii. Any person who is granted forest rights under the Schedule Tribes and other Traditional Forest Dwellers(Recognition of Forest Right) Act 2006 act 2 of 2007 or under any other law for the time being in force; or

iii. Who is entitled to be granted Patta rights on the land under any law of the State including assigned lands; or

iv. Any person who has been declared as such by an order of the court or Authority.

Schedule-II of the RFCTLARR Act, 2013 vide clause (b) of Sl. No. 4 makes a provision for annuity of one time payment of Rs 5 lakhs per affected family




and section 3 (c)(i) refers to a family which is qualified by the expression whose land or other immovable property has been acquired. The person whose land or other immovable property has been acquired would be the land owner. This “ land owner” is a person whose name is recorded as the owner of the land in the records of the authority.

Therefore, there would be two categories of persons included as families –

a. Recorded land owner on the date of acquisition/vesting of land along with his or her spouse, minor children, minor brothers and minor sisters dependent on him;

b. "Separate family" as per the proviso appended to section 3(m) would include widows, divorcees and women deserted by families. Even as per the explanation to section 3(m), an adult of either gender with or without spouse or children or dependents are to be considered as a separate family.

Upon conjoint reading of all the provisions/definition clauses, the recipient of benefit as would be available under the $II^{nd}$ Schedule are only the affected families. Affected families who are entitled to receive would include land owner and the persons covered by separate family as well as the persons who fall under the category of section 3(c)(ii), (iii), (iv) and (v) whose livelihood were linked with the lands acquired.

Any adult person who is out of the definition of family u/s 3(m) claiming relationship with the land owner cannot be considered as an affected family as he remains unconnected with the land of the family and accordingly his claim of being affected due to loss of livelihood is not established.

Thus one time payment of Rs 5 lakhs is to be paid to the recorded owner of the land i.e land owner which includes himself, his spouse, minor children, minor brother and minor sister dependent on him constituting a family. Further, in case where the recorded owner of the land is not alive, his widow and in case of divorce the divorcee etc along with minor children and dependents would constitute one family entitled to one time payment of Rs 5 Lakhs.













Therefore, in the event of acquisition of land under CBA(A&D) Act, 1957 the living recorded land owner on the date of vesting of land in the Central Government (i.e the dated of Notification u/s 9 of the CBA(A&D) Act, 1957 along with his dependents or in case of his death, his widows and divorcees etc will constitute one unit of recipient of the benefits. No other adult relatives of the recorded land owners by claiming their relationships with the recorded land owners would be entitled to the benefit by treating them as separate affected families.

To conclude, I am of the firm OPINION that only the land owner on the date of vesting along with his dependents categorized in section 3(m) alone constituting one unit would be receiving the benefit. No adult relative of the recorded land owner claiming through the recorded landowner would be entitlement of onetime payment of Rs 5 Lakh. To illustrate Adult Brother, Adult Sister, Uncle(Chacha, Fufa), Aunt (Chachi, Fufi) etc would not be entitled to the payment of Rs 5 lakh as they do not fall under the category of affected family. It is only the recorded land owner on the date of vesting u/s 9 who would be entitled to onetime payment for the "affected family".

In respect to other category of acquisition where land along with homestead and/or other assets attached to the land have been acquired the same principle as above would apply as per the statutory provisions namely the benefits, year marked for displaced families in Schedules of RFCTLARR Act, 2013.

This is my opinion.

#  OPINION

1. The Central Government acquired certain lands under the provisions of the Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition and Development) Act, 1957 (hereinafter referred to as 'the Act'). By virtue of Section 10(1) of the Act, land or the rights in or over the land have vested absolutely in the Central Government. By virtue of Section 11(1) of the said Act, the lands as well as the rights in or over the land were vested in a Government company called the Central Coalfields Limited. By virtue of provisions of Section 3(1) of the Coal Mines (Nationalization) Act, 1973 from the appointed date right, title and interest of an owner in relation to the coal mines vested absolutely in the Central Government. Section 10(2) of the Act refers to a case where the rights under a mining lease granted by the State Government to an individual are acquired under the 1957 Act. It is only pursuant to such acquisition that the Central Government could be deemed to have become the lessee of the State Government. The expression 'vest absolutely in the Central Government' used in Section 10(1) is distinct from 'be deemed to have




becomes the lessse of the State Government' under Section 10(2) of the Act. By virtue of Section 18A of the Act, an express overriding provision has been incorporated, by which the Central Government may pay to the State Government such sum of money as would have been payable as 'royalty' by a lease had such land or rights been under a mining lease granted by the Statement Government'. The expression 'royalty' which occurs in Sector 18A of the Act is not a tax. Royalty is the rent of the land on which the mine is situated or the price of the privilege of winning the minerals from the land parted by the Government in favour of mining lessce. 'The concept of 'surface tent' is not one which is included within the expression 'royalty'. The expression 'royalty' is the payment for use and benefit of the minerals excavated from an area. This is distinct from the 'surface rent' which is with reference to the area demised or 'dead rent' which is calculated on the basis of the area leased.

2. The expression 'surface rent' as used in Rule 27(1)(d) of 'The Minerals Concessions Rules, 1960 is not capable of being construed as 'royalty' within meaning of Section 18A of the Act.





3. In the circumstances, in view of the above, the demand made by the State of Jharkland upon the querist to pay 'surface rent' is not supported by Section 18A of the Act. Apart from royalty payable under Section 18A of the Act, the relationship of a lessor and lessee which would arise between the Central Government Company and the State Government by virtue of Section 10(2) of the Act does not arise when vesting has taken place under Section 10(1) of the Act. In view of the vesting having taken place by virtue of Section 10(1) of the Act, the relationship between lessor and lessee would not be in place and in existence. However, notionally, such relationship is in existence only to the limited extent provided by the overriding provisions contained in Section 18A of the Act which permits nationally collection of 'royalty'. I have also noted that payment of Royalty is being made to the State Government under Section 18A of the Act.

4. In view of the above, the claim for 'surface rent' by the State Government of Jharkhand is not maintainable.

The burden to prove that the Suit Property was the self-acquired property of Mangal Kumhar was on the Appellant – Purchasers. Reliance is placed on this Court’s judgment in Adiveppa & Ors. v. Bhimappa & Ors.,$^{1}$ wherein it was held that :–

"22. The Appellants have failed to discharge the burden to prove that the suit property was separate or self-acquired property of Mangal Kumhar.

5. The contention raised by the Appellants is that since Mangal Kumhar was the recorded tenant in the suit property as per the Survey Settlement of 1964, the suit property was his self-acquired property.

The said contention is legally misconceived since entries in the revenue records do not confer title to a property, nor do they have any presumptive value on the title. They only enable the person in whose favour mutation is recorded, to pay the land revenue in respect of the land in question.$^{2}$

As a consequence, merely because Mangal Kumhar's name was recorded in the Survey Settlement of 1964 as a recorded tenant in the suit property, it would not make him the sole and exclusive owner of the suit property.




6. The Appellants have failed to adduce any evidence whatsoever, apart from the Survey Settlement of 1964 to establish that the suit property was the self-acquired property of Mangal Kumhar.

7. Since Mangal Kumhar did not have an exclusive right, title or interest in the suit property, his widow Etwari Kumharin was not legally competent to sell the suit property to the Appellants, purporting to be the sole owner of the property.

Reliance is placed on Eureka Builders & Ors. v. Gulabchand & Ors.,$^{3}$ wherein this Court held :–

"40. It is a settled principle of law that a person can only transfer to other person a right, title or interest in any tangible property which he is possessed of to transfer it for consideration or otherwise.

41. In other words, whatever interest a person is possessed of in any tangible property, he can transfer only that interest to the other person and no other interest, which he himself does not possess in the tangible property.

42. So, once it is proved that on the date of transfer of any tangible property, the seller of the property did not have any subsisting right, title or interest over it, then a buyer of such property would not get any right, title and interest in the property purchased by him for



#  www.LIVELAW.IN

consideration or otherwise. Such transfer would be an illegal and void transfer."

(emphasis supplied)
